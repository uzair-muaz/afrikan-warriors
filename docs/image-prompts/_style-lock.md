# Afrikan Warriors — image style lock

**Reference grade:** the six client stills in `public/media/v1/source/01-pyramid.jpg` … `06-freedom-vocal.jpg`.  
Match those before anything else. Paste this lock with every prompt.

## Brand

Live international African theatre & circus photography — **real production stills**, not fashion editorials, not black-box studio voids, not safari clichés.

Afrikan Warriors = Tanzanian-born touring company. Athletic, cultural, theatrical, premium. Think West End / circus-theatre documentary photography shot mid-show.

## What the PDF stills teach (match this)

1. **Full stage, not empty void** — mats or dark stage floor, props in shot (large cylindrical drums, drum kit, saxophone, ladders, baskets, draped canvas, lighting stands).
2. **Visible lighting hardware** — side lanterns on stands, overhead truss, warm beams cutting haze. Multiple sources, not one cone in blackness.
3. **Rich theatrical haze colour** — warm amber/gold key light **plus** magenta / purple / pink atmospheric wash in the deep background is allowed and desirable (see extract-01). Not neon cyberpunk — stage gels.
4. **Costume colour is alive** — teal/turquoise satin performance trousers, leopard/animal-print unitards, vibrant patterned wraps (orange, red, earth), radial stick/feather headpieces, heavy bead collars, face paint when story needs it. Not only muted raffia-on-black.
5. **Ensemble energy** — musicians and supporting cast visible; formation reads as a company, not a lone model.
6. **Expressive performance faces** — open mouths singing/shouting, joy, intensity. Not blank fashion stares. Not fake stock-photo smiles.
7. **Painted or draped scenic backdrops OK** — jungle/waterfall flats (Tokomile), canvas mask cloths, tent-like rustic drapes. Still clearly a theatre stage.
8. **Modern + traditional contrast OK** when storytelling calls for it (e.g. grey suit beside traditional wraps — extract-02).
9. **Photoreal, sharp, high detail** — sweat, fabric weave, skin texture. Slight motion energy on limbs is fine; no CGI plastic skin.

## Palette

- Stage / void: deep matte black `#0B0B0B`
- Key light: warm theatrical amber / gold `#D4AF37` / `#f2ca50`
- Atmosphere: optional magenta–purple haze wash behind subjects
- Costume: teal satin, leopard gold/black, terracotta, burnt orange, copper, beadwork cream
- Skin: realistic East African / Tanzanian tones with highlight from stage lamps

## Theatre (required)

Build a **working stage**:

- Dark stage floor or interlocking performance mats
- Thick haze catching beams
- Multiple warm lanterns (front key + sides); truss or stands visible if natural
- Props that belong to Afrikan Warriors shows: drums, percussion, rustic drapes
- Optional painted scenic flat or canvas drop for story productions
- Audience only if it helps the wide hero — not mandatory on every frame

## People

- East African / Tanzanian adult performers, athletic, real bodies
- Barefoot unless the act needs otherwise
- Mix of men and women where the act/show includes both
- Costumes theatrical and production-ready (see PDF colour notes above)
- Performance energy: focus, power, celebration — never tourist cliché

## Camera

Photoreal still. 35mm / 50mm theatre photography. Sharp subjects, haze in beams, crushed blacks in wings, natural falloff. High contrast, not HDR plastic. Widescreen heroes 16:9; act tiles often 3:4.

## Do not

No text, letters, logos, watermarks, UI, captions.  
No extra limbs, fused hands, broken pyramids, extra fingers.  
No cartoon, anime, illustration, 3D render, plastic CGI skin.  
No empty cyclorama / one-spotlight sports studio void.  
No fantasy armor, superhero costumes, chrome, neon cyberpunk.  
No wildlife, savannah stock, tourist “tribal Halloween”.  
No identifiable real celebrities.

## Negative prompt (copy with every generation)
